# The Unbroken Sky story bible

Version 1.2 ? Current setting: futuristic fallen kingdom. Companion to FINAL_SCRIPT.md

## Decisions now settled

The catastrophe arose from a coercive public infrastructure system, concealed exemptions, dangerous cost cutting and a rescue undertaken despite known risks. No single revelation makes every other cause disappear. The protagonist was a child subjected to the system, not its secret architect. His adult choices determine whether he reproduces its logic. Mera rescued him but previously helped administer the harm and retained evidence that should have belonged to him. She must answer for both.

The central human conflict is between immediate security and accountable cooperation under unequal power. The intended continuation ending establishes a difficult, monitored alternative. It does not discover that all religion is false or erase material scarcity. The protagonist keeps his eagle form and loses borrowed abilities when he releases the binding.

## Logline

When the disaster that destroyed a prosperous river capital reaches his dry homeland, a young courier seeks a water fortress's surviving keeper and discovers that his mother rescued him from the system that sustained the fallen city. His ability to become an eagle makes him a target for those who want to restore that system—and the person best placed to help replace it.

## Setting

The game is a futuristic African-inspired fantasy about an advanced kingdom after its fall, with no asserted Gregorian date, historical map or real kingdom called Zambia. The owner's 28 October 2026 direction supersedes the earlier precolonial visual baseline. Maamba and the southern Zambian landscape are starting points for later visual and environmental research, not claims that Maamba was a historical desert kingdom. The landscape includes dry woodland, rocky escarpments, cultivated ground and seasonal water; avoid depicting the whole region as sand desert.

Red Hollow belongs to a smaller upland kingdom. It inherits developed housing, fabrication and service infrastructure, including an older independent water network with manual emergency pumps. Its less wealthy districts are crowded, unevenly maintained and improvised, rather than technologically undeveloped. Its people know difficult routes and trade engineering and metalwork with river neighbours. Their initial relative safety is geographic and infrastructural. Heat tolerance is acquired experience and practical knowledge, not ethnic superhuman biology. They are neither culturally uniform nor morally purer than the capital.

The river alliance includes multiple polities. Only one ruined capital is visited. Its farming, engineering, medicine, scholarship and craft are genuine human achievements. Magical extraction subsidised some services and luxury consumption; it did not create African civilisation. This distinction is essential to the premise.

Architecture is to be developed from specific regional references, credited by source. Do not collage Malian mosque forms, Zambian sacred masks and Egyptian monuments into one allegedly authentic city. The present script uses functional spaces and invented institutional symbols until design research is complete.

## The faiths

The House of Continuance maintains a covenant theology: the living inherit duties towards predecessors and successors. Its local attendants shelter travellers, preserve testimony and maintain public sites. Its central council has acquired dangerous authority to interpret obligations on others' behalf. Orun embodies the tension; Lio and Nali demonstrate religious service outside domination.

Hearth observances are diverse household traditions involving remembrance, hospitality and duties to place. People disagree about who counts as an ancestor, whether inherited promises remain binding and how obligations may be withdrawn. No single fictional practice stands in for all real Zambian religion.

The Open Hand is a small reform tendency, not a foreign missionary religion: a promise must be renewable, witnessed and limited. Its ideas survived in maintenance rules and marginal notes, which is why the relay alternative is recovered knowledge rather than one hero inventing ethics.

Some gods or ancestral experiences may remain genuinely beyond explanation. The covenant's machinery being corrupt does not disprove the sacred. Religious disagreement survives the ending.

## Rules of magic

1. Life energy is finite. Magic changes where costs fall; ordinary labour, food, metal and water still matter. Minor consented offerings cause temporary fatigue. Permanent vessel use destroys autonomy over time. We never convert these into a grind currency bought with money.
2. A name identifies a party to an existing ritual agreement; speaking any stranger's name does not enslave them. Binding requires a prepared channel, recognised authorising access and witnesses or an already corrupted substitute authority. This is why offices and control points matter.
3. Contracts can be abused, but a document is not omnipotent. Destroying a paper copy does not erase a person. Copper controls and independent records are needed to change the covenant. Administrative language is an interface, not reality itself.
4. Recognition can let a counterfeit presence claim access within a prepared site. Away from those sites, knowing someone's face is harmless. A new name alone never provides universal magical protection.
5. Shapeshifters are rare people with an innate capacity to cross bodily forms. This predisposition was exploited by the vessel programme; state violence did not create their humanity or their natural gifts.
6. Each shifter has one native form. Tavo's is the eagle. Kesi's is the hyena. Sena has not chosen to explore hers in this game. Unknown does not mean absent. No cultural group is assigned a morally good or bad animal.
7. The forced network can carry fragments of memory and senses. Controlled live sharing requires agreement. Old involuntary leakage is treated as a violation, not romantic spiritual unity. It is neither omniscience nor a reliable lie detector.
8. Borrowed forms require a living, consenting source connected to the network. Tavo borrows only Kesi's hyena form. Dead traces cannot grant new powers or be brought back as whole people.
9. Severance ends live sharing and removes memories carried only by the link. Memories independently formed or written down remain. Tavo does not forget everything the player learned. Recovered knowledge survives through records and witnesses.
10. The Claimant repeats material carried by the channels. It cannot know an unheard private thought or offer objectively verified messages from the dead. Its use of Ilen's letter is explicitly a replay, not confirmation of her fate.
11. The ancient wound produces dangerous accumulations if not contained. Central containment has high capacity but one coercible point of control. The distributed relays have lower capacity, separate shutoffs and finite local contributions. Their advantage is resilience and accountable access, not infinite free energy.
12. A safe alternative needs all three relays, technical knowledge, protected participation and reciprocal control. Saying the right words alone cannot produce it.

## Chronology

Dates are relative to the opening. These are author facts, not early player knowledge.

- Generations ago: a limited, renewable covenant contains the wound beneath the river. Several independent stations and shared oversight are part of its design.
- Over generations: rulers centralise access. Exemptions increase. Provincial representatives are permitted to sign without meaningful household agreement. The House's central council suppresses dissenting records.
- Seventeen years ago: Tavo, seven, and Kesi, ten, are connected in the vessel programme. Mera registers Ilen's petition. Later she takes Tavo through a service door and falsifies his death record. The temporary loss of a child is compensated by burdening others; his escape alone does not cause the catastrophe.
- Fourteen years ago: Mera returns and rescues Sena, five. Tavo and Sena grow up as siblings. Neither knows their adoption or previous binding. Mera suppresses evidence and keeps the children's past from them.
- In the last year: Veya approves an expansion without investigating its hidden lower channels. A council including Orun approves closing peripheral release channels to save material and maintenance costs.
- Six weeks before opening: the renewal is intended to make the remaining five child recruits, now adults, permanent vessels. Kesi breaks free, leads a rescue and learns the side channels are sealed. With guards returning, she opens the centre. Two of the five survive, three die. The accumulated load returns through the city. Mera's supplied route was obsolete. The capital falls.
- Following weeks: river authorities blame upland interference. Trade and relief fracture. Coercion and retaliation worsen the damage. The disturbed network reaches secondary channels.
- Opening day: Red Hollow's separate well becomes contaminated through a deeper feeder channel. Its earlier isolation delayed the threat, not prevented it.
- Game: several days, with travel transitions. Water release buys time. Evidence, relationships and preparation enable the final containment choice.

## Character engines

### Tavo

Wants: bring water home and clear his family's name. Needs: distinguish love from innocence; use power without claiming other people's lives. False belief: if he saves his own, he can leave other claims behind. Worst credible temptation: become the benevolent centre and decide for everyone. Agency: never loses player control because another mind takes possession. Humour: practical exaggeration, especially with Sena and Daru. Avoid modern political slogans and all-purpose wisdom.

Secret progression: strange memories → Mera's office connection → childhood captivity → adoption and Ilen → mother's part in coercion → live network → cost of its release. He was not secretly royalty. Ordinary origins matter.

### Mera

Wants: keep both children safe and keep being their mother. Needs: give them their histories and accept accountability she does not control. Worst act: converting a parent's plea into an extraction record. Best act: rescuing children at real personal risk. Her love is not a cover story. Her rescue is not absolution. She is neither executed for catharsis nor instantly forgiven by the script.

### Sena

Wants: become a skilled maker and determine what happens to her body. Has independent insight into the network. She helps solve the engineering problem and sets terms for release. She is not kidnapped to supply motivation, sacrificed for Tavo's growth or required to forgive exposure of her identity. Her humour challenges him rather than flattering him.

### Daru

Wants: keep people at home alive. Rational concern: agreements can collapse while his community pays first. Blind spot: treats outsiders as uncounted liabilities. His argument exposes the similarity between Mera's selective rescue and his own selective protection. His development requires verifiable reciprocal support, not one moving speech.

### Nali

Wants: keep refugees alive and retain their political voice. Loves genuine achievements of the capital. Blind spot: may mistake local reluctance for malice when others are also frightened. She accepts practical compromise while refusing to trade survivors' locations for legitimacy. She never exists only to educate Tavo; her clinic and community continue without him.

### Veya

Wants: restore functioning infrastructure and preserve continuity of rule. Knows more engineering than anyone present. Blind spot: confuses specialised competence with a right to govern. She must surrender reserve access and submit her own signed orders to the same scrutiny she demands of Mera. Her ending is a useful role without automatic coronation.

### Orun

Wants: stop an actual supernatural disaster. Faith teaches service as well as obedience. He feeds critics and names uncertainty, yet still believes concentrated authority is the only dependable answer. The player may oppose him without denying his good works. He acknowledges his council role. Lio's willingness never licenses sacrificing Tavo or Sena.

### Kesi

Wants: prevent return to captivity and reach the last distant survivor. Needs: accept that being wronged does not make all rescue methods harmless. Her choice during the catastrophe was made under immediate threat; this context matters but does not nullify casualties. She refuses to make Tavo guilty for a promise made at seven. She retains her own form after severance.

## Story craft commitments

Use goals before lore. Every scene must alter an objective, a relationship or the interpretation of evidence. The prologue pays off through a viewpoint change, not an invented event. Physical records corroborate subjective memories. Reveal the final cost before the irreversible choice. Give daily care and comedy room between horrors. Do not make every character an aphorism machine; actors and text-only testing should remove lines that sound like speeches rather than responses.

The most important interpretation remains open: how much Tavo can forgive Mera. The machinery's cause is established; the moral judgement belongs to the player. No author speech declares that a particular present-day ethnic, religious or political group represents the villains.

## Prose and game continuity

FULL_STORY.md follows joint emergency repair, protected testimony, shared gate control, accountability with Mera and relay containment. The game supports alternatives without changing the underlying history. Mera's wrist burn is an occupational injury, not evidence that she was a vessel. The three-cut marks belong to recruited vessels, but marks alone are not treated as sufficient proof of a person's identity. Tavo retrieves Sena's copper bird after his first flight. His initial weapon is his repair hammer. The fourth living vessel is an adult outside the map whose consent is obtained through the already established connection. Native forms remain after release; borrowed forms and link-only memories do not.
