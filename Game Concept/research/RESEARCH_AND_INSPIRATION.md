# Research and inspiration register

Research date: 26 October 2026. This is targeted desk research for a fictional game, not fieldwork, a comprehensive literature review, or community validation. Source links identify what was actually consulted. Publicly available summaries and articles do not confer permission to reproduce restricted knowledge, performances, images or translations.

The full text of Black Leopard, Red Wolf was not supplied or consulted for this project. Its publisher description and an author interview informed the broad creative reference. No passage, dialogue, named character, fictional map or distinctive plot sequence has been adapted. This is not an authorised adaptation or a claim to reproduce Marlon James's writing style.

## Zambia and the region

### R01 National symbols of Zambia

Source: State House, National Symbols. https://www.sh.gov.zm/national-symbols/

The eagle is part of Zambia's national imagery. This supports the user's choice of an eagle as a contemporary cultural point of connection. The fictional hero's form and its personal significance are our invention. We do not place the modern flag or national identity unchanged in an invented ancient society. No assertion is made that every historical Zambian community assigned the eagle one meaning.

### R02 Sacred landscapes in the Gwembe Valley

Source: Lilian Cheelo Siwila, 2015, An encroachment of ecological sacred sites and its threat to the interconnectedness of sacred rituals, Journal for the Study of Religion 28(2). Full public article consulted. https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1011-76012015000200007

The article connects sacred places, ecological relationships, ritual and political pressure in a specific Tonga context. Design inference: losing a place can mean losing relationships and obligations as well as infrastructure. It informs the game's attention to who bears development costs. The fictional life levy, binding and supernatural catastrophe are not Tonga beliefs. We do not reproduce the article's ritual procedures. This is a situated scholarly interpretation, not a single definitive account of all Tonga people.

### R03 Nyaminyami and Kariba

Source: Zambia Tourism, History of the Building of Lake Kariba. https://www.zambiatourism.com/destinations/lakes/lake-kariba/history/

This public tourism account associates Nyaminyami with a particular river landscape. It is not sufficient evidence for a complete ancient cosmology. Kariba's dam history is modern and colonial; it is not the game's historical setting. The source helps identify a boundary: the fictional Claimant must not be marketed as Nyaminyami or used to turn a living belief into a generic boss. The script invents its own wound and covenant.

### R04 Makishi masquerade

Sources: UNESCO listing and public explanatory records. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/makishi-masquerade-00140 ; https://museum.unesco.org/stolen-objects/VM-ZM-25-003_Samahongo_mask/

Public UNESCO records place Makishi within particular communities, including Luvale, Chokwe, Luchazi and Mbunda, and identify ceremonial significance. Access limitation: the direct listing returned a browser challenge; indexed public text and the accompanying museum record were available. Design decision: do not extract sacred mask characters as monsters or invent an allegedly authentic initiation. We draw only the general lesson that performance and spiritual authority can have social functions beyond horror.

### R05 Gule Wamkulu

Sources: UNESCO public listing and multimedia description. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/gule-wamkulu-00142 ; https://www.unesco.org/archives/multimedia/document-634

The public record locates this tradition among Chewa communities across Malawi, Zambia and Mozambique. It is distinct from Makishi. The listing page itself returned a challenge; the public indexed and multimedia descriptions informed this narrow note. No restricted meanings, costumes, dances or rites are included in the script. The supernatural maintenance husks are entirely invented and must not be given sacred masquerade designs during art production.

### R06 A useful chronological exclusion

Source: UNESCO, Kalela dance. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/kalela-dance-01698 ; committee decision https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/17.COM/7.b.35

UNESCO describes Kalela as originating during colonial times. It therefore cannot simply be labelled an unchanged ancient practice in this setting. No Kalela sequence is scripted. This is an example of why research must check dates rather than assume any recognisably Zambian practice belongs to every period.

## Central African history and memory

### R07 Luba memory boards

Source: Metropolitan Museum of Art, Lukasa memory board, object 1977.467.3. https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/690570

The record describes a specific relationship among material mnemonic devices, political history and trained interpretation. Design inference: authoritative memory is socially maintained and can check power as well as support it. Our archive is a fictional copper hydraulic record system, not a lukasa that literally stores minds. The script does not reproduce the board's designs or secret interpretive content. Luba traditions are not presented as an undifferentiated Zambian religion.

### R08 Sacred kingship and councils

Source: Alexander Ives Bortolot, 2003, Kingdoms of the Savanna The Luba and Lunda Empires, Metropolitan Museum of Art. https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/kingdoms-of-the-savanna-the-luba-and-lunda-empires

The essay discusses sacred kingship, council authority, exchange and political adaptation in particular Central African histories. Its seventeenth and eighteenth century discussion has a different chronology from medieval Mali and includes external trade. Design inference: sacred authority need not mean a monarch without constraints. Our fictional House, council and gate agreement are invented; we do not claim to reconstruct Luba or Lunda statecraft.

## Other African storytelling and history

### R09 Sunjata and living oral versions

Source: Hakimah Abdul-Fattah, 2020, How Griots Tell Legendary Epics Through Stories and Songs in West Africa, Metropolitan Museum of Art. https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/sahel-sunjata-stories-songs

This account describes the Sunjata epic as transmitted in multiple oral versions and situates griots within specific West African practices. Design inference: differences in a story's telling can reveal audience, responsibility and authority. We use the repeated doorway scene to change viewpoint while preserving events. We do not copy a modern translation or transpose the epic's characters and plot. No Zambian character is labelled a griot as though that were a universal African office.

### R10 Mali and Mansa Musa

Source: Metropolitan Museum of Art, Sahel Art and Empires on the Shores of the Sahara, exhibition material. https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/exhibitions/sahel-art-empire-sahara/inside-the-exhibition

The exhibition distinguishes multiple Sahelian polities and explores interactions among Islamic and other traditions, scholarship, authority and material culture. Mansa Musa's pilgrimage belongs to fourteenth century Mali, not ancient Zambia. Design inference: institutions can be intellectually and socially sophisticated while containing rival sources of authority. The game uses an original religious framework and does not disguise Islam as a malicious fantasy cult or import a historical ruler as its antagonist.

### R11 Christianity and the setting boundary

Source: Metropolitan Museum of Art, African Christianity in Ethiopia. https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/essays/african-christianity-in-ethiopia

Christianity in Africa predates European colonialism, including the fourth century adoption associated with Aksum. The fictional game's lack of Christianity is a chosen setting feature, not a claim about all precolonial Africa. No Christian church is scripted. The House of Continuance is an original institution with its own theology, social service and internal disagreement.

## The literary reference

### R12 Black Leopard Red Wolf

Sources: Penguin Random House publisher page. https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/561305/black-leopard-red-wolf-by-marlon-james/9780735220188/ ; Penguin interview, 14 February 2019. https://www.penguin.co.uk/discover/articles/marlon-james-interview-black-leopard-red-wolf

The book's broad reference value is African grounded fantasy, a dangerous supernatural world and morally complicated people. In the interview James discusses character, geography, research and narrative voice. Design inference: build characters before spectacle, and let research challenge assumptions. We use original dialogue and a distinct family and infrastructure plot. The script's lack of romance reflects this game's scope; it is not a statement that gay characters are inherently inappropriate or that African societies are culturally uniform.

## Collective action and production

### R13 Common resources and governance

Sources: Elinor Ostrom, 2009 Nobel lecture landing page, https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/ ; Ostrom, 1999, Coping with Tragedies of the Commons, author abstract, https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.polisci.2.1.493 ; Indiana University research record, https://dlc.dlib.indiana.edu/dlc/items/8d0e56e2-6c3e-4075-a1d9-431c85702402

These sources motivate examining institutions and cooperation rather than assuming a resource problem has only private ownership or central command as solutions. Access limitation: the Nobel lecture PDF returned 403, so no claim is based on reading its full text. Our gate choices, separate controls, visible records and repair paths are fictional design applications, not a reproduced formal model. No universal optimum, behavioural experiment or guaranteed player effect is claimed.

### R14 Godot and modest hardware

Sources: official Godot licence https://godotengine.org/license/ ; official stable requirements https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/about/system_requirements.html

Godot is available under the MIT licence and can be used commercially subject to applicable notices. Its documentation distinguishes renderers and emphasises testing the actual project on modest hardware. Recommendation: start with a 2D Compatibility project, without assuming that the engine's published baseline guarantees our performance. No engine installation or game build was performed for this narrative package.

### R15 Creative independence

Source: WIPO copyright FAQ. https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright

General ideas and protected expression are different; adaptations are among uses rights holders may control. Project decision: do not rename and reproduce the novel's cast, locations or story. This is a creative boundary informed by general guidance, not jurisdiction-specific legal clearance. The working game title has not been cleared as a trademark.

## Evidence versus invention

Researched: the existence and distinct contexts of the named practices, histories and works above. Inferred: the creative lessons explicitly marked as design inferences. Invented: every scene, dialogue line, character, fantasy institution, magical rule, covenant atrocity and game location in the project.

No consultation with a community custodian, historian, linguist or author is claimed. Before public release, seek appropriately credited, compensated local review when feasible. If budget cannot support detailed consultation, keep sacred designs and ceremonies out of the game and use the original symbols already specified. Community participation must not be invented in the credits.
